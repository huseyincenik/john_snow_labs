PATIENT: Patient
ID: p01

REASON FOR CONSULTATION: Personal history of multiple primary cancers for evaluation of hereditary cancer syndrome.

BACKGROUND: This is a 64-year-old gentleman with a remarkable personal history of multiple malignancies, including:
- Rectal adenocarcinoma in 1987 (age 32)
- Appendiceal adenocarcinoma in 2005 (age 50)
- Prostate adenocarcinoma in 2015 (age 60)
- Newly diagnosed high-grade urothelial bladder cancer in 2019 (age 64)

FAMILY HISTORY: Significant for father with colon cancer and a sister with metastatic cancer of unknown primary.

ASSESSMENT: The patient's personal and family history are highly suggestive of Lynch Syndrome (Hereditary Non-Polyposis Colorectal Cancer). His constellation of tumors (colorectal, appendiceal, urothelial, prostate) fits the spectrum of Lynch-associated malignancies. We discussed the implications of this diagnosis, including the need for lifelong surveillance and the potential risk for his family members.

PLAN:
1.  Proceed with germline genetic testing via a multi-gene panel (JSL-IMPACT) to identify a causative mutation. We specifically discussed looking for mutations in mismatch repair (MMR) genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2).
2.  Patient provided informed consent for testing.
3.  Will schedule a follow-up visit to discuss the results and their implications for his future care and for his family.
