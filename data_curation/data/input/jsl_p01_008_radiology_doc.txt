EXAMINATION: CT UROGRAM W/WO CONTRAST
CLINICAL STATEMENT: 64-year-old male with history of Lynch syndrome, prior pelvic radiation, and prostate cancer. Recent gross hematuria and 0.8 cm bladder lesion on prior MRI. Evaluate for urothelial carcinoma.

TECHNIQUE: Multiphase CT imaging of the abdomen and pelvis was performed without and with intravenous contrast, including non-contrast, nephrographic, and excretory phases.

COMPARISON: Abdominal MRI from 2018.

FINDINGS:
KIDNEYS/URETERS: No hydronephrosis or hydroureter. No filling defects in the upper tracts. Bilateral simple renal cysts are noted.

BLADDER: There is a new, avidly enhancing soft tissue nodule in the left posterior bladder wall, near the trigone. It measures 1.2 x 1.1 cm in maximal dimensions. This corresponds to the lesion seen on the prior MRI and has increased in size. The bladder wall is otherwise mildly trabeculated, consistent with post-radiation changes.

PROSTATE: Status post radiation changes. Fiducial markers are present.

OTHER: Status post-APR with end colostomy. No abdominopelvic lymphadenopathy. Degenerative changes in the spine.

IMPRESSION:
1.  A 1.2 x 1.1 cm enhancing nodule in the left posterior bladder wall, suspicious for urothelial carcinoma.
2.  No evidence of upper tract urothelial disease.
3.  No pelvic lymphadenopathy or distant metastatic disease.

RECOMMENDATION:
Cystoscopy with biopsy is recommended for definitive diagnosis.
