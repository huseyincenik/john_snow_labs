SUBJECTIVE:
Patient is a 66-year-old male with a complex GU oncologic history (prostate ca s/p RT, bladder ca s/p TURBT/BCG, Lynch syndrome) seen for follow-up. His left ureteral stent was removed 6 weeks ago. He feels well. No urinary complaints. His primary concern today is his rising PSA.

OBJECTIVE:
Labs:
- PSA (10/21/2021): 1.65 ng/mL (Previous: 1.11 in Mar 2021, 0.97 in Oct 2020)
- Testosterone (10/21/2021): 351 ng/dL

Cystoscopy (today, 10/28/2021): Urethra is patent. Bladder shows diffuse post-radiation changes. The prior tumor site on the posterior wall is well-healed. The left UO is patent and appears normal. No evidence of recurrent tumor.

ASSESSMENT:
1.  **Prostate Cancer (C61.9):** Confirmed biochemical recurrence. The PSA is now >1.5 ng/mL and has been steadily rising, with a doubling time of <12 months. This is concerning for recurrent disease.
2.  **Bladder Cancer (C67.9):** No evidence of disease. Surveillance cystoscopy is clear.
3.  **Lynch Syndrome:** Stable.

PLAN:
1.  **Prostate Cancer Recurrence Workup:** Given the PSA kinetics, it is time to proceed with advanced imaging to localize the site of recurrence.
    - Order PSMA-PET/CT scan.
    - Order a dedicated multi-parametric MRI of the prostate.
    - The goal is to determine if this is a local (prostate bed) recurrence, which may be amenable to salvage therapy, versus distant metastatic disease.
2.  **Bladder Cancer:** Continue surveillance cystoscopy and urine cytology every 6 months.
3.  Follow up in clinic after imaging is completed to discuss results and formulate a treatment plan.
