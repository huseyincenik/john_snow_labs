This note serves as an integrated summary of the patient's oncologic status at the end of 2025.

OVERALL STATUS: The patient is a 70-year-old male with Lynch Syndrome who is living with multiple, complex, and chronic oncologic conditions. He is currently stable and doing well, with all malignancies either in remission or well-controlled.

CURRENT ONCOLOGIC STATUS BY PRIMARY:

1.  **Prostate Cancer (C61.9):** Local recurrence diagnosed in 2022. Currently well-controlled with continuous Androgen Deprivation Therapy (Relugolix). PSA remains suppressed at <0.05 ng/mL. He is being managed for ADT-related osteopenia with Denosumab.

2.  **Bladder Cancer (C67.9):** High-grade non-muscle invasive disease diagnosed in 2019. He remains with no evidence of disease (NED) after TURBT and induction BCG. He has passed the 5-year mark and is now on annual surveillance.

3.  **Rectal Cancer (C20.9):** Diagnosed 1987. Considered cured. No evidence of recurrence.

4.  **Appendiceal Cancer (C18.1):** Diagnosed 2005. Considered cured. No evidence of recurrence.

ACTIVE ISSUES & MANAGEMENT:
-   **Lynch Syndrome (MSH2):** This remains the central driver of his medical journey. He is adherent to a comprehensive, multi-specialty surveillance program (Urology, GI, Derm, Oncology).
-   **Chronic Left Ureteral Stricture:** A long-term complication of his prior treatments. This is successfully managed with routine (every 6 months) internal ureteral stent exchanges.
-   **ADT Side Effects:** Osteopenia is being managed with Denosumab, Calcium, and Vitamin D. He experiences mild hot flashes.

SUMMARY:
Mr. [Patient] exemplifies the modern challenge and success of managing a patient with a hereditary cancer syndrome. Through intensive, coordinated, multidisciplinary care, his multiple malignancies are well-controlled, and he maintains a good quality of life. The plan is to continue all current therapies and surveillance protocols into the new year.
