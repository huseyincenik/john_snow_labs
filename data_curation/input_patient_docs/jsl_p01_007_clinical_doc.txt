SUBJECTIVE:
Patient is a 64-year-old male, s/p EBRT (75.6 Gy) and ADT for prostate cancer, completed in April 2016. He has a history of Lynch syndrome with prior rectal and appendiceal cancers. He presents for routine follow-up.
He reports feeling well overall. However, he notes an episode of gross hematuria approximately one month ago. This was a single episode, which self-resolved. He was seen at a local clinic and given antibiotics, though no formal diagnosis was made.
An MRI of the abdomen, done for his routine colon cancer surveillance in late 2018, incidentally noted a "0.8 cm lesion along the posterior bladder wall." He has not had specific follow-up for this finding yet.
His PSA has been slowly rising but remains low.

OBJECTIVE:
Labs:
- PSA (02/02/2019): 0.23 ng/mL (Previous: 0.11 in Jan 2018)
- Testosterone (02/02/2019): 374 ng/dL (Recovered)

ASSESSMENT:
1.  **Prostate Cancer (C61.9):** Biochemical recurrence (BCR). His PSA is slowly rising from its nadir of <0.05. The doubling time is long, and the absolute value is still very low. This is consistent with a local recurrence.
2.  **New Bladder Lesion / Hematuria:** The combination of gross hematuria and an enhancing bladder lesion on MRI in a patient with Lynch Syndrome and a history of pelvic radiation is highly concerning for a new primary bladder cancer (urothelial carcinoma). This requires urgent evaluation. The history of pelvic radiation is a known risk factor, as is Lynch Syndrome itself.

PLAN:
1.  **Prostate Cancer:** Continue active surveillance with serial PSA monitoring every 6 months. No intervention is indicated at this time for the slow BCR.
2.  **Bladder Lesion:**
    - Urgent referral back to Urology (Dr. Johnson).
    - Recommend CT Urogram to evaluate the entire urinary tract.
    - Recommend urine cytology.
    - He will almost certainly require a cystoscopy with biopsy/resection of the bladder lesion.
3.  Reinforced the importance of the urologic evaluation given his multiple risk factors for urothelial carcinoma.
