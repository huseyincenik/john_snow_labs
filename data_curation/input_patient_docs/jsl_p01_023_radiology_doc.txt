EXAMINATION: MRI PROSTATE POST TREAT W/WO CONTRAST
CLINICAL STATEMENT: 66-year-old male with Lynch syndrome, s/p radiation for prostate cancer in 2016. Rising PSA of 1.65 ng/mL. Evaluate for recurrence.

TECHNIQUE: Multiplanar, multisequence images of the prostate were obtained at 3T without and with intravenous gadolinium contrast.

COMPARISON: MRI from October 01, 2015 (pre-treatment).

FINDINGS:
The prostate gland is small (volume 9 cc) with diffuse post-radiation changes. Surgical clips from the prior APR cause significant artifact.

PROSTATE LESIONS:
There is an area of abnormal enhancement in the posterior right peripheral zone, from mid-gland to apex. This area demonstrates T2 hypointensity and restricted diffusion. It measures approximately 1.6 cm. This corresponds to a site of known tumor on the pre-treatment scan but now shows features suspicious for viable, recurrent tumor in the post-treatment setting.

EXTRACAPSULAR EXTENSION (ECE): No definite ECE is identified.

SEMINAL VESICLES (SV): Not well visualized due to artifact.

LYMPH NODES: No pelvic lymphadenopathy.

BONES: No suspicious osseous lesions.

IMPRESSION:
1. Findings in the posterior right peripheral zone are suspicious for a local recurrence of prostate cancer.
2. No evidence of metastatic disease in the pelvis.
3. Correlation with PSMA PET/CT is recommended.
